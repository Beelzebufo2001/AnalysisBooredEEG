_residual_correlations — the most important step. Uses Huber regression (robust to outlier bridges contaminating the fit) with features [dist, dist², 1/(dist+1)] to model the natural distance-decay of correlation. Everything downstream works on the surprise, not raw correlation. This alone kills most false positives from naturally close electrodes.

_build_graph — density-invariant by design. Uses top-percentile globally, not a fixed threshold. So 1005 and 1020 use the same parameters without retuning. Also requires both percentile AND z-score to pass, so isolated noise spikes don't slip through.

_prune_spatial_chains — your "spatially impossible bridge" idea. For any flagged edge (i→j), checks whether any intermediate electrode k exists within chain_gap_ratio * d_ij of both endpoints. Short edges between true neighbors bypass this check (they don't need a chain). Only long-range weird edges get pruned.

_prune_triangle_inconsistency — your transitivity idea inverted. Instead of detecting transitive bridges as one object (the clustering does that), this prunes edges that have no triangle support AND aren't extreme enough to justify being a lone spike. A real gel blob produces a dense local subgraph, not a random long edge.

_temporal_slope — 10s sliding windows over the early recording, linear regression on the mean pairwise correlation trajectory. Positive slope = correlation building over time = gel spreading. Flat = anatomical. This feeds into the confidence score with 20% weight.
BridgeCluster — one object per gel blob, not an edge list. Confidence is a weighted combo of density (50%), spatial compactness (30%), temporal slope (20%). You can retune these weights trivially.

One thing to check when you run it: chain_gap_ratio and edge_percentile will need tuning for your actual 1005 data — start with percentile around 97–98 for that density.
